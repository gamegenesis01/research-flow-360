The big story

The Fed holds rates steady and signals one cut next year

The Federal Reserve left its benchmark rate unchanged on Wednesday, as expected. The surprise was in its projections: officials now see one rate cut next year, down from two in September.

The chair said inflation has cooled but "not convincingly enough" in services, and that the job market remains solid. Stocks dipped on the news, then recovered by the close.

Why it mattersFewer cuts means borrowing costs stay higher for longer: mortgages, car loans and credit cards are unlikely to get much cheaper soon.

Source: Federal Reserve statement and projections

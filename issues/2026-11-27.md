## The big story

### Wall Street fell hard on Wednesday, then took most of it back

The last full session before Thanksgiving started as a rout and ended as a near miss. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell more than 1,100 points in the morning, the S&P 500 fell 3.2% at its low and the Nasdaq Composite fell 4.6%, before buyers stepped in late in the day. By the close the Dow was up 0.33% at 50,182.95, while the S&P 500 was down just 0.25% at 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq was down 0.62% at 23,585.80. Most S&P 500 stocks actually rose on the day, even though the biggest technology names dragged the indexes down.

Three things hit at once. The Bureau of Economic Analysis revised third quarter growth up to a 4.3% annual rate, from a first estimate of 3.4%, and reported core PCE inflation, the Federal Reserve's preferred measure that leaves out food and energy, at 3.3% over the year to October, up from 3.1% in September and above the 3.2% expected. The White House also proposed a new round of tariffs, and chip stocks sold off again, with Nvidia down 3.9% to $149.30. The 10-year yield, the interest rate on 10-year US government bonds, eased about 6 basis points to 4.85%, and markets were then closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving.

> Why it matters: Strong growth with inflation rising again is the mix that keeps borrowing costs high. It helps explain why futures markets now price a December rate increase, not a cut, and why a handful of large tech stocks can pull the whole index around even on a day when most stocks rise.

## Quick hits

### [Washington] New tariffs proposed on China, the EU and 12 other partners
The administration proposed tariffs on 14 trading partners under Section 301, with China at 20%, which could rise to 40% if the current trade truce is not extended, and the European Union at 15%, which could rise to 20%. The proposal follows the Supreme Court striking down an earlier round of global tariffs, and the new levies would not take effect until after a January 25 hearing, at the earliest in January.
Source: [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/11/25/trump-tariffs-china-european-union-midterm-election-inflation/92956667007/)

### [Fed] Futures price a December hike as the likely outcome
Fed funds futures on November 26 priced about a 96% chance of a 25 basis point increase at the December 8 to 9 meeting, with the current target range at 3.75% to 4.00%. That pricing followed three straight monthly core PCE readings of 0.3%, which ING said all but seals a 25 basis point hike on December 9.
Source: [Frenzy Capital, Fed funds futures pricing](https://www.frenzycap.com/fedwatch/2026-12-09?utm_source=openai)

### [Earnings] HP jumps, Deere rises, and the chip slide narrows
HP Inc. rose 11.5% to $31.15 after reporting fourth quarter revenue of $16.0 billion, up 9.3%, and giving fiscal 2027 earnings guidance of $3.15 to $3.45 a share. Deere rose 4.4% to $641.02 after reporting fourth quarter net income of $1.051 billion and forecasting fiscal 2027 net income of $4.50 billion to $5.25 billion, while chip names including Lam Research, Micron and Nvidia finished lower but well off their morning lows.
Source: [Smart Money Watch, stock movers for November 25](https://www.smwmarkets.com/biggest-stock-movers-today-hp-ambarella-nutanix-nov-25/)

### [Energy] Brent holds near its November high on Hormuz and Saudi attacks
Brent crude was at $96.19 a barrel early on Friday, after settling at $96.57 on Thursday, its highest close since November 6, while US WTI crude was at $85.41, up 2.07% from Wednesday's close with no Thursday settlement due to the holiday. Reuters reported continued attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and on Saudi energy sites, and said OPEC+ is likely to keep its output targets for at least the first quarter of 2027 when ministers meet on Sunday.
Source: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/brent-trades-near-months-high-mideast-conflict-threatens-supply-2026-11-27/)

### [Global] Asian shares mostly higher in Friday morning trading
Japan's Nikkei 225 added 0.7% to 57,919.30 in morning trading, South Korea's Kospi rose 2.2% to 4,812.65, Hong Kong's Hang Seng edged up 0.4% to 22,310.11 and Australia's S&P/ASX 200 rose 0.4% to 8,800.50, while the Shanghai Composite slipped 0.1% to 3,473.40. Tokyo core consumer prices rose 2.9% over the year in November, up from 2.6% in October.
Source: [Associated Press via the Los Angeles Post](https://www.lapost.com/content/asian-shares-mostly-rise-ahead-of-wall-street-s-reopening-after-the-thanksgiving-holiday)

### [Retail] Black Friday opens a holiday season forecast to pass $1 trillion
The National Retail Federation forecasts November and December holiday sales will grow 4.2% to $1.013 trillion, the first time its measure passes $1 trillion, and expects 182.7 million people to shop from Thanksgiving through Cyber Monday. Adobe Analytics forecasts $275.1 billion in online holiday spending, including $12.9 billion on Black Friday and $15.1 billion on Cyber Monday, and forecasters including Visa note that higher prices account for a large share of the expected growth.
Source: [XTB summary of the NRF and Adobe Analytics forecasts](https://www.xtb.com/en/market-analysis/black-friday-2026-analysis-these-6-stocks-could-benefit-from-the-shopping-boom)
